# Product Owner assessment (written before reading the others)

1. **Already efficient.** Golden days written before implementation turned acceptance into runnable tests. The innovation Sprint found the clo-matching gap, the most valuable finding in three Sprints.
2. **Inefficient.** Sprint Goals were sized to "five items" because there were five Developers. Several items were small enough for one agent in one pass (Sprint 6 #81, Sprint 7 #88). PO decisions arrived as chat and were re-typed into docs.
3. **Wrong assumptions.** Merging PO into one lead is fine for this product (one stakeholder, one backlog), but the stakeholder is the real PO: the lead should escalate product trade-offs (privacy of push data, "feels like" wording) instead of deciding alone. I decided both alone.
4. **Underused.** The ordered backlog: we planned Sprints from the latest idea rather than from value order. US-080 (match in clo) sat unknown because nobody tested the full outfit against the target.
5. **Not yet known to the stakeholder.** Most of our cost is reading, not writing. Cutting what each agent reads saves more than cutting agents.
6. **Risks.** A lead-only model for "small changes" skips peer review; correctness bugs found in review (°F, NaN, zero exposure) were all in small changes.
7. **Change.** Accept the single lead, but keep two explicit gates the lead cannot waive: an independent review of every change, and stakeholder sign-off on privacy or user-visible meaning changes. Plan by value order with a token budget per Sprint.
